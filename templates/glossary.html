What each term on the dashboard means, and how it shapes the prediction.

Market Data Inputs
Coal / Oil / Iron Ore / Natural Gas Prices
Real monthly commodity prices sourced from the World Bank's Pink Sheet. Coal and Iron Ore are the actual cargo being shipped; Oil and Natural Gas represent fuel-related energy costs.
Why it matters: Higher commodity prices generally signal more shipping demand, and fuel prices directly affect a vessel's voyage cost — both feed into the freight cost prediction.
USD/INR
The US Dollar to Indian Rupee exchange rate, sourced from the Frankfurter API (real daily rates).
Why it matters: Freight is priced in USD, but Indian importers pay in INR. A weaker rupee makes the same USD freight cost effectively more expensive for the importer — a real currency risk factor.
VIX (Volatility Index)
Known as the market's "fear gauge" — a real, widely used index measuring expected volatility in financial markets, sourced from Yahoo Finance.
Why it matters: When VIX is high, markets are jittery and unpredictable — this often coincides with freight rate instability too, so it's used as a general risk signal.
WTI (West Texas Intermediate)
A major global crude oil price benchmark, separate from Brent crude, sourced from Yahoo Finance futures data.
Why it matters: A second independent oil price signal — futures prices reflect market expectations, not just current spot price, adding a forward-looking element.
SBLK (Star Bulk Carriers Stock Price)
The real stock price of Star Bulk Carriers, one of the world's largest dry bulk shipping companies, sourced from Yahoo Finance.
Why it matters: Shipping company stock prices reflect real-time investor sentiment about the freight market — a genuine market-based signal, not a synthetic one.
MA3 (Moving Average)
The average of a value over recent periods, smoothing out short-term noise to reveal the underlying trend.
Why it matters: A single period's price can spike due to one-off events; the moving average gives the model a steadier signal of where the market is actually heading.
Volatility
The statistical standard deviation of a price over recent periods — a measure of how much it's been swinging up and down.
Why it matters: High volatility in oil or coal prices signals an unstable market, which raises the risk of unexpected freight cost changes — feeds directly into the Risk Score.
Prediction & Model
BDRY (Breakwave Dry Bulk Shipping ETF)
A real, publicly traded fund (ticker: BDRY) that tracks dry bulk freight futures — essentially a live, tradeable proxy for the famous Baltic Dry Index, which itself is not freely available.
Why it matters: This is the model's actual prediction target — a real, independent market value, not something calculated from our own inputs. This avoids circular logic in the model.
Predicted BDRY
The model's estimate of where the BDRY index value should be, given the market conditions you entered.
Why it matters: This single number drives the entire vessel cost calculation — it's converted into an estimated daily charter rate for each vessel type.
Confidence Range (±RMSE)
RMSE (Root Mean Squared Error) is how far off the model's predictions typically are, measured on real historical test data. The range shown is the prediction ± this margin.
Why it matters: No model is perfectly accurate — showing the honest margin of error is more trustworthy than presenting a single number as if it's guaranteed exact.
R² (Model Accuracy Score)
A statistical score (0 to 1) showing how well the model's predictions match real outcomes. Closer to 1 means better fit.
Why it matters: This is the standard way to report whether a prediction model is actually working, not just guessing.
Random Forest
The machine learning algorithm used, selected after comparing it against Linear and Ridge Regression via cross-validation on a dataset of 400 real weekly data points.
Why it matters: Shows the model choice was deliberate and evidence-based, not arbitrary.
Cross-Validation
A technique where the model is tested on 5 different random splits of the data (not just one), and the results are averaged.
Why it matters: Confirms the model's accuracy is stable and not just a lucky result from one particular train/test split.
SHAP
A technique that explains exactly how much each input feature contributed to a specific prediction, in either direction.
Why it matters: Makes the model's reasoning transparent rather than a black box — and was used to catch and remove a flawed feature (calendar Year) that was inflating accuracy artificially.
Forecast
3-Month Forecast Avg
The average predicted BDRY value over the next 3 months, calculated using ARIMA — selected after backtesting five different forecasting methods.
Why it matters: This is what lets the system answer "is the market going up or down," not just "what's the price right now."
Projected Change (%)
The percentage difference between the current BDRY value and the 3-month forecast average.
Why it matters: Directly drives the "book now vs. wait" recommendation — a rising trend suggests booking sooner, a falling trend suggests waiting.
Trend (Rising / Falling / Stable)
A simple label derived from the Projected Change — Rising if forecast is meaningfully higher, Falling if lower, Stable if roughly flat.
Why it matters: Translates a raw percentage into an instantly understandable market signal.
Risk & Vessel Decision
Risk Score
A composite score (0-100) combining Oil Volatility, Coal Volatility, and VIX, weighted together into one number, then bucketed into Low/Medium/High.
Why it matters: Gives a single, digestible risk signal instead of making the user interpret three separate volatility numbers themselves.
Vessel Market Availability
A derived indicator (Low/Moderate/High) based on where the predicted BDRY falls in its historical range. This is NOT live vessel tracking — real-time vessel position data requires paid AIS tracking services (like MarineTraffic), which aren't freely available.
Why it matters: High BDRY (high freight rates) typically means vessel supply is tight — so this reframes a real market signal as an availability proxy, transparently labeled as derived rather than live-tracked.
DWT (Deadweight Tonnage)
The real, standard shipping industry measure of how much cargo weight a vessel can carry — this is what "vessel capacity" actually means in shipping.
Why it matters: Directly determines how many trips are needed to move your cargo quantity, and therefore total cost and delivery time.
CII (Carbon Intensity Indicator)
A real regulatory rating (A through E) introduced by the IMO (International Maritime Organization) that rates a vessel's carbon emissions efficiency. A = best, E = worst.
Why it matters: Increasingly relevant for compliance and cost — vessels with poor CII ratings may face operational restrictions or higher costs in the near future.
Draft / Feasibility Check
Compares a vessel's real draft (how deep it sits in water) against a port's real maximum draft limit. Kolkata specifically requires lighterage (transfer to smaller vessels) due to its shallow riverine approach.
Why it matters: Prevents recommending a vessel that physically cannot dock at the chosen port — a real, documented operational constraint for India's East Coast ports.
Route & Tariff
Nautical Miles (Route Distance)
Real distance between origin and destination ports, calculated using the haversine formula from actual port coordinates.
Why it matters: Determines voyage days and fuel consumption, which directly drive vessel cost calculations.
BCD (Basic Customs Duty) / IGST
Real Indian import tax categories — BCD is the base import duty, IGST is the Integrated Goods and Services Tax applied to imports. For coal and iron ore, BCD is typically 0% and IGST is 5%.
Why it matters: These are real added costs on top of freight that affect the total landed cost of imported cargo.
